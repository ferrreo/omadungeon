# Environment tilesets

Generated by `python3 tools/art/gen_all.py` (module `tools/art/tiles.py`). Never hand-edit
the PNGs; edit the generator and re-run. All five sheets share one layout so the rooms
module can address tiles by `(column, row)` regardless of biome.

## Palette contract (8 colours only)

Every environment tile and every prop in `assets/sprites/props/<biome>.png` is drawn with the
*authoring ramp* and nothing else. A palette-swap shader maps each index to a
`ThemePalette` role, so any other colour would leak through untinted.

The index is **not** a free shade choice: `TileRamp.target_colors()`
(`src/rooms/tile_ramp.gd`) gives each one a fixed `ThemePalette` role, so a floor painted in
index 2 comes out in the theme's *wall* colour.

| Index | Key | RGBA | Theme role | Use it for |
|-------|-----|------|------------|------------|
| 0 | `.` | transparent | — | nothing |
| 1 | `o` | `#101010` | `void` | outlines, mortar, deep shadow |
| 2 | `d` | `#303030` | `wall` | **wall faces**, pit lips, dark detail |
| 3 | `m` | `#505050` | `floor` | **floor base**, prop bodies |
| 4 | `l` | `#707070` | `floor_alt` | floor detail / decorated floors, lit prop faces |
| 5 | `h` | `#909090` | `wall_top` | **wall caps** and the lit top edge of a wall face |
| 6 | `A` | `#b00000` | accent A (rolled from the theme's prop pool) | lava, book spines, danger runes |
| 7 | `B` | `#0000b0` | accent B | ice cracks, magic, glitch |

Because of that mapping the generator keeps three invariants, and
`tests/unit/art/art_assets_test.gd` enforces them (counting only the structural shades
2-5, so outlines and accent decals do not decide the role):

* each plain floor variant (row 0, columns 0-3) is dominated by index 3,
* every wall cell that shows a **face** (mask without the S bit) is dominated by index 2
  below the cap band,
* each wall cap (row 2) is dominated by index 5.

Biome character therefore comes from the *patterns* (flagstones, riveted plate, sheet ice,
shelves, scanlines) and from the two accents — never from picking a different shade for a
floor. The same test fails if any tile pixel is outside the eight colours.

## Sheet layout — `assets/tiles/<biome>.png` (256 x 80, 16 x 16 cells, 16 columns)

Biomes: `crypt`, `forge`, `frost`, `library`, `void`.

| Row | Columns | Content |
|-----|---------|---------|
| 0 | 0-3 | floor variants (plain) |
| 0 | 4-7 | decorated floor variants: surface marks only (stains, cracks, seams, moss, a rug strip, glitch) |
| 1 | 0-15 | **wall autotile**, indexed by the 4-bit mask of neighbours that are also walls: `N=1, E=2, S=4, W=8`. A cell whose south neighbour is *not* a wall shows the brick/panel **face**; other cells show the wall **top** with an outlined edge on every open side. |
| 2 | 0-3 | wall top / cap variants (free-standing pillars, decorative caps) |
| 3 | 0-15 | **pit autotile**, indexed by the 4-bit mask of neighbours that are also pits (same bits). Open sides get a lip in the floor colour plus a shadowed inner wall. |
| 4 | 0 | door, closed |
| 4 | 1 | door, open |
| 4 | 2 | stairs down |
| 4 | 3 | stairs down, locked (boss / key gate) |
| 4 | 4 | altar |
| 4 | 5 | shop counter |
| 4 | 6 | shrine |
| 4 | 7-8 | wall torch, 2 animation frames (flame in accent A) |
| 4 | 9-12 | wall lanterns the lighting layer hangs on the generator's anchors (`WallLantern`): cage lantern, brazier, candelabra, crystal cluster; flame or facets in accents A/B |

Mask example: a wall with walls to the north and west only has mask `1 + 8 = 9` -> row 1,
column 9. Diagonal neighbours are not encoded.

Tiles are drawn for a top-down 3/4 view: walls are solid, the face row belongs to the wall
cell (not the floor cell below it), so a 1-tile-thick wall reads as a low parapet.

## Reading the world at a glance

The first design pillar is readable chaos, and the first thing a player has to read on a
floor is *which of these squares is which*: scenery they may smash, a tile that will hurt
them, and a box worth opening. Colour cannot carry that distinction on its own - the
environment ramp is recoloured at runtime from whatever theme the desktop is wearing, and a
theme is free to make the floor, the props and the accents any hue it likes. So the grammar
is **coverage and frame**, which survives every palette, light or dark, and reads in
greyscale:

| Class | Footprint | Frame | Palette |
|-------|-----------|-------|---------|
| **Clutter** - props, breakables, set dressing | **Inset.** Never paints the tile's outer 1 px ring. A solid stands on a baked contact shadow inside an ink outline; a flat has neither. | none | the 8-colour ramp; the theme owns its colour |
| **Hazard** - every trap, pit and plate | **Full-bleed.** Paints all four tile edges: a hazard *is* the floor. | `props.hazard_frame`: corner brackets pointing inward, amber when it damages you, cold blue when it only moves you | its own colours, never retinted |
| **Reward** - chests | Inset, a free-standing box: wide, low, a lid seam and a centre latch | a gold band, plus the white glow cell modulated to the theme's `loot` | its own wood and gold, never retinted |

Two traps deliberately break the hazard row, and only two: `mimic_chest` and `ricer_trap`.
Being mistaken for something else is their whole mechanic (docs §9, §7), so they wear no
warning frame. `tests/unit/rooms/art_grammar_test.gd` asserts all of it against the shipped
PNGs - including that those two are the *only* unframed hazards - so a new prop that fills
its tile, or a new trap that does not, fails the build rather than the player.

Two rules follow for everything else drawn on a floor:

* **A decorated floor variant is a mark on the floor, never a thing on it.** The crypt's
  decorated cells used to be a skull with ember eyes, a candle, a jar and a cobweb; one floor
  tile in eight wore one, in the middle of the fight space, with no collision and no place in
  the prop catalogue - and the owner read them as "confetti I can't identify". Floor detail
  is a stain, a crack, a seam, a patch of mould or a worn stone: no ink outline (the solid
  prop's tell), no bright object shape, accent pixels only as the tint of a stain. Anything
  that is a *thing* belongs in `assets/sprites/props/` under the solid-or-flat rule.
* **A decorated floor variant may not wear a closed bright border.** A rug or a grate painted
  as concentric rings is the hazard signature, and a player who learns to walk around a
  carpet has learned the wrong lesson. Floor detail is texture: stripes, scorch, cracks,
  scatter.
* **Floor detail may not wear a hazard's shape.** The frost biome's floor crystals used to be
  a row of points rising off the tile, which is exactly what a spike plate looks like; they
  lie flat now.

## Other environment art

* `assets/sprites/props/<biome>.png` — `Prop.KIND_COUNT` (9) props per biome (ramp colours,
  16 x 16 cells, 144 x 32: row 0 is the prop, row 1 = `Prop.DEBRIS_ROW` is what a solid kind
  leaves behind when smashed). The column order **is** `Prop.KINDS[<biome>]` / `prop_kinds` in
  `data/biomes/<biome>.tres` and `data/rooms/rooms_content.tres`; the generator writes the same
  list to `tools/art/prop_kinds.json`, which the unit test compares against all of them. The
  first four columns are the kinds every biome shares (`Prop.SHARED`); the other five are the
  biome's own.

  | Biome | Columns 0-8 (flat kinds in *italics*) |
  |-------|-------------|
  | all | barrel, crate, sack, *rubble* |
  | `crypt` | coffin, gravestone, urn, candle, *bones* |
  | `forge` | anvil, cart, brazier, *chain*, *slag* |
  | `frost` | ice_block, lantern, snowman, boulder, *snow* |
  | `library` | bookshelf, desk, lectern, globe, *books* |
  | `void` | crystal, monolith, floater, orb, *rift* |

  **Why this small.** The catalogue was 27 kinds and the owner reported three times that the
  clutter "makes little sense": every kind was a grey rounded rectangle with a different texture
  inside it, all one size and - once the room tint landed - all one hue. Nine a biome, each with
  a silhouette of its own (tall, round, square, wide, pointed) and one identifying feature in the
  accent, is what a player can name in a glance; the fourth pass cut cobweb, furnace, ice patch,
  pages and glitch because each read as another kind's texture. The rules the maps are drawn
  to are at the top of `tools/art/prop_maps.py`; every map is drawn in-house (no third-party
  art, nothing generated from a grammar or noise).

  **Solid or flat.** Every kind is one of two things, and the drawing says which (the third
  time clutter was reported, the finding was "some has collision but others don't?? it is very
  random" - the sprites gave no way to tell):

  * *Solid* - an upright object. Drawn tall (from row 4 or above), wrapped in a 1 px **ink
    outline** (at least `INK_MIN` ink pixels), three tones with one feature, and standing on a
    **contact shadow**: a run of ink on row 14, the last row inside the border ring. It blocks
    movement, takes hits, casts a shadow (a `LightOccluder2D` the size of its base, hidden
    once it breaks) and breaks into its row-1 debris frame (chunks of its own body strewn flat,
    collision gone). Heavy kinds (`Prop.STURDY`) take two hits.
  * *Flat* - lying on the floor. Starts on row 4 or below, never paints row 14, and carries
    **no ink at all**: two tones plus an accent, so it is lower in contrast than anything
    upright. Walk-over decoration: no collider (its shape is disabled), no occluder, no hits,
    never breaks; its row-1 cell is empty.
    A solid's debris frame follows the same recipe, ink included, because debris is walk-over
    too.

  The table lives in one place per layer and every layer is held to the others:
  `tools/art/props.py::SOLID` authors it and refuses a drawing that contradicts it
  (`_assert_recipe`), `tools/art/prop_solid.json` is its export, `Prop.SOLID` mirrors it in
  code and `prop_solid` in `data/rooms/rooms_content.tres` is the one the game reads
  (`Prop.is_solid`). `tests/unit/rooms/prop_test.gd` reads the shipped PNG back against all
  of them (ink, shadow, height, debris), and pushes a `CharacterBody2D` at a barrel and at
  bones to prove the physics. `tests/unit/rooms/prop_render_test.gd` then measures the
  *edge* of every kind - the ink ring of a solid, the outer pixels of a flat - against the
  floor on all six theme fixtures, dry and under the room's torches.

  **Where they stand** is `PropPlacement` (`src/rooms/prop_placement.gd`): solids in clusters
  of two or three against walls and in corners, flats sparse in the open, nothing on a door
  apron or on the shortest route between two doors. The numbers are `prop_*` in
  `rooms_content.tres`.
* `assets/sprites/props/chest.png` — chest in real colours: `closed, open, glow, wobble`.
  The `glow` frame is a white ring (alpha) to be modulated with the theme `loot` colour.
* Traps (`assets/sprites/traps/<kind>.png`) use real greys/metals so they stay readable on
  any theme; the danger tell should be added by the trap scene (modulate to `danger`).
  Frame order matches `TrapBase`: 0 idle, 1 telegraph, 2 active (extra frames are recovery).

  | `fire_vent.png` | 4 (vent, smoking, flame, tall flame) |
  | `pressure_plate.png` | 2 (raised, pressed) |
  | `ice_slide.png` | 1 |
  | `laser_grid.png` | 3 (emitters, warm-up beam, full beam) |

## Rooms-module contracts that touch this art

* **Layers.** `FloorBuilder` builds `Ground` / `Pits` / `Walls` TileMapLayers from one runtime
  TileSet over the sheet above. A room with `palette_variant != 0` gets its own
  `Ground_r<id>` / `Pits_r<id>` / `Walls_r<id>` trio so its whole footprint (pits included)
  shares one palette-swap material.
* **Prop columns.** `Prop.setup()` picks the sprite by kind *index* while hp/`Prop.STURDY`
  and solidity/`Prop.SOLID` come from the kind *name*, so the art and the data must agree
  column for column. They do:
  `tools/art/props.py::PROP_TABLE` is authored in `Prop.KINDS` order (table above) and
  exported to `tools/art/prop_kinds.json` for the test. Adding a kind means editing the map
  (`prop_maps.py`), `PROP_TABLE`/`SOLID`, `Prop.KINDS`/`SOLID`, `rooms_content.tres` and
  `data/biomes/<biome>.tres` - and swapping one out, because `KIND_COUNT` is fixed at 10.
* **Overriding tables without code.** `RoomsContent` (`src/rooms/rooms_content.gd`) is an
  optional `res://data/rooms/rooms_content.tres` that overrides `Chest.WEIGHTS`,
  `Shrine.DEFAULT_OPTIONS` and `Prop.KINDS`; without the file the script constants apply.

## Item, pickup and FX sheets

`assets/sprites/items/*.png` cells are addressed by `AtlasTexture` regions that are already
committed in `data/items/<id>.tres` (`region = Rect2(cell * 16, 0, 16, 16)`), so the drawing
order in `tools/art/items.py` is a contract. `tools/art/item_cells.json` pins it and the unit
test checks every item resource against it.

| Sheet | Cells |
|-------|-------|
| `items/weapons.png` | 0 rusty_sword, 1 longsword, 2 axe, 3 spear, 4 dagger, 5 shortbow, 6 crossbow, 7 wand, 8 staff, 9 throwing_knives, 10 golden_cane, 11 fists, 12 greatsword, 13 executioner_axe, 14 glaive, 15 stiletto, 16 longbow, 17 archmage_staff, 18 runed_staff, 19 chakrams. Cells 0-11 are the ones `WeaponController.ATLAS_INDEX` draws in the hand (a family word maps there); 12-19 exist so every weapon has an inventory icon of its own, which is what the chest card, the pickup and the pause page show. |
| `items/armor.png` | 0 leather_jerkin, 1 shadow_weave, 2 chain_mail, 3 scale_hauberk, 4 iron_plate, 5 adamant_plate |

Three sheets are generated and pinned by the test but have **no consumer yet** — flagged here
so the cell order does not drift before someone wires them up:

* `assets/sprites/pickups.png` — 0-3 gold coin spin, 4 heart, 5-10 stat orbs in
  `Stats.PRIMARY` order (vitality, might, precision, arcana, swiftness, fortune).
  `src/entities/pickups/pickup_base.gd` currently draws coins/hearts/orbs procedurally.
* `assets/sprites/fx/particles.png` — 0 dust, 1 spark, 2 confetti, 3 smoke (8x8 art centred in
  16x16 cells), for `CPUParticles2D.texture`.
* `assets/sprites/ui/cursor.png` — 16x16 crosshair, hotspot (8, 8).
